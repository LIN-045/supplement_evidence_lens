Antioxidants

(PDF Version - 132 K)

This monograph is intended to serve as a guide to industry for the preparation of Product Licence Applications (PLAs) and labels for natural health product market authorization. It is not intended to be a comprehensive review of the medicinal ingredients.

Notes

  • Text in parentheses is additional optional information which can be included on the label at the applicant's discretion.
  • The solidus (/) indicates that either term and/or statement may be selected on the label.
  • When "Fruit" is indicated as source material, it only includes the parts of the fruit that are edible and consumed historically as human food.
  • This monograph may be used to support single ingredient or multi-ingredient products containing any medicinal ingredient from Table 1. Combination restrictions are described in the ‘Ingredient Combinations’ section.

According to the principles outlined in the Guidance Document: Classification of products at the food-natural health product interface: products in food formats, beverages are classified as foods based on product representation to consumers, product format, public perception and history of use. Please be advised, applications for products in liquid dosage forms with single doses greater than 90ml may be subject to additional product classification review.

    Ingredient Combinations

    All ingredients included in this monograph may be combined together with the following restrictions

    • alpha-Lipoic acid: No permitted combinations between DL-alpha-Lipoic acid and R-alpha-Lipoic acid.
    • beta-Carotene/Vitamin A: The maximum daily dose for beta-carotene (including beta-carotene from mixed carotenoids) in combination with other vitamin A source materials must not exceed the maximum daily dose for vitamin A indicated on the NNHPD MVM monograph. The conversion factor of beta-carotene into vitamin A is 6:1. This factor is also applied to mixed carotenoids.
    • alpha-Tocopherol/Vitamin E: The maximum daily dose expressed in mg AT (mg alpha-tocopherol) for combination of mixed tocopherols, tocotrienols and/or vitamin E must not exceed the maximum daily dose for vitamin E indicated on the NNHPD MVM monograph. The amount in mg for mixed tocopherols and/or mixed tocotrienol is considered as equivalent to mg AT for the purpose of this combination rule and for determining the dose at which Vitamin E risk statements would be required for Class I and II applications (as there is no fixed conversion that can be established at this time). Data on the mg AT content of specific mixed tocopherols and/or mixed tocotrienol preparations may be submitted at Class III for precise calculations if desired.
    • Vitamins/Minerals: The total daily dose of each vitamin and mineral must not exceed quantities listed in the NNHPD MVM monograph.
    • Fruit/vegetable blends: The medicinal ingredients ‘fruit blend’ and/or ‘vegetable blend’ cannot be combined with the medicinal ingredient ‘fruit and vegetable blend’. When fruits and vegetables are blended, the medicinal ingredient ‘fruit and vegetable blend’ should be selected.
    • Blueberries: The various species of Blueberries (Vaccinium angustifolium; Vaccinium corymbosum; Vaccinium pallidum), can be combined if the total quantity of dried fruits, fresh fruits, fresh fruit juice, dried fruit juice or concentrated fruit juice does not exceed the values listed in Table 2. Dried and fresh fruits cannot be combined as part of this monograph.
    • Spirulina: The various species of Spirulina (Limnospira fusiformis; Limnospira indica; Limnospira maxima; Limnospira platensis) can be combined if the total quantity does not exceed the values listed in Table 3.

    Cannabis sativa seeds (Hemp)

    This monograph is for Cannabis sativa seeds from hemp only. Hemp is defined in the Industrial Hemp regulations (IHR) as "a cannabis plant — or any part of that plant — in which the concentration of THC is 0.3% w/w or less in the flowering heads and leaves". Approved hemp cultivars are set out in the List of Approved Cultivars, published by the Government of Canada on its website, as amended from time to time. Additional cultivars may be permitted such as those on the Association of Official Seed Certifying Agencies (AOSCA) list which also meet the definition of Hemp set out in the IHR.

    Cannabis sativa seeds (Hemp) must not contain more than 10 parts per million delta-9-Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), or phytocannabinoids that have been isolated or concentrated, or synthetic duplicates of phytocannabinoids. The determination of the THC concentration must take into account the potential to convert delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinolic acid (THCA) to THC.
