GERMAN CHAMOMILE - MATRICARIA CHAMOMILLA

Buccal

(PDF Version - 53 KB)

This monograph is intended to serve as a guide to industry for the preparation of Product Licence Applications (PLAs) and labels for natural health product market authorization. It is not intended to be a comprehensive review of the medicinal ingredient.

Notes

  • Text in parentheses is additional optional information which can be included on the labels at the applicant's discretion.
  • The solidus (/) indicates that either term and/or statement may be selected on the label.

Date

May 29, 2026

References: Proper name: USDA 2025; Common names: USDA 2025, Gardner and McGuffin 2013; Source information: Mills and Bone 2005; ESCOP 2003; Blumenthal et al. 2000; WHO 1999; Bradley 1992.

Route of administration

Buccal (ESCOP 2003; Blumenthal et al. 2000; Bradley 1992)

Dosage form(s)

Acceptable dosage forms when used according to the requirements indicated in this monograph: Gargle; Loose; Mouthwash; Powder

Use(s) or Purpose(s)

Used in Herbal Medicine to help relieve minor inflammation and/or irritation of the mucous membranes of the mouth and/or throat (ESCOP 2003; Bradley 1992; Blumenthal et al. 2000).

Dose(s)

Subpopulation(s)

Children 6-11 years; Adolescents 12-17 years; Adults 18 years and older (Bove 2001; Schilcher 1997)

Quantity(ies)

Note: On the PLA form, quantities can be expressed as percentage weight by volume (% w/v) or percentage volume by volume (% v/v), depending on the product formulation. On the product label, quantities can also be expressed in other equivalent concentration units (e.g., mg/mL).

Loose/Powder dosage forms

Methods of preparation: Dry, Powdered

100% dried flowers (Mills et Bone 2005; ESCOP 2003; Blumenthal et al. 2000; OMS 1999; Bradley 1992).

Note: Powdered and dried flowers must be prepared as an infusion or decoction by the consumer prior to use (see direction for use) and must provide an equivalent of 3 to 10 grams dried flowers per 100 milliliters of finished product (Mills and Bone 2005; ESCOP 2003; Blumenthal et al. 2000; WHO 1999; Bradley 1992).

Methods of preparation: Non-Standardized Aqueous Liquid Extracts (Decoction, Decoction concentrate, Infusion, Infusion concentrate)

3 - 100% w/v of dried flower extract preparation in the finished product (Mills and Bone 2005; ESCOP 2003; Blumenthal et al. 2000; WHO 1999; Bradley 1992).

Note: The extract ratio must be between 1:1 and 1:33. The formulation must be prepared in a way which is equivalent to a quantity of 3 to 10 grams crude dried flowers per 100 milliliters of finished product. For example, for an infusion prepared with a 1:10 w/w ratio, the concentration of infusion in the final formulation must be 30 to 100% (3 - 10 g crude dried flowers * 10 w/w (dilution) = 30 - 100 g liquid extract in 100 mL finished product = 30 - 100% w/v extract preparation in the finished product).

Method of preparation: Non-Standardized Ethanolic Liquid Extracts (Fluid extract, Tincture)

1 - 5% v/v of dried flower extract preparation in the finished product (ESCOP 2003; WHO 1999).

Note: The extract ratio must be between 1:1 (fluid extract) and 1:5. The formulation must be prepared in a way which is equivalent to a quantity of 1 gram crude dried flower per 100 milliliters of finished product. For example, for a tincture prepared with a 1:5 w/w ratio, the concentration of tincture in the finished product must be 5% (1 gram crude dried flowers * 5 w/v (dilution) = 5 milliliters liquid extract in 100 milliliters finished product = 5% v/v extract preparation in the finished product).

Direction(s) for use

Loose/Powder dosage forms

  • Place [insert volume to be measured by consumer, e.g., X teaspoons] of dried flowers in [insert the volume of water, e.g., 1/2 cup (125 mL)] of boiling/cold water. Infuse for [e.g., 5-15 minutes]/Bring to a boil and let simmer for [e.g., 10-20 minutes] and strain.
  • Use 1 tablespoon (15 mL) as a mouthwash/gargle, as needed.

Note: The direction for use statements can be modified on the label provided it gives clear instructions to consumers. In addition, “1 tablespoon (15 mL)” can be replaced on the label with an equivalent unit of measure.

Gargle/Mouthwash dosage forms

Use 1 tablespoon (15 mL) as a mouthwash/gargle, as needed.

Note: “1 tablespoon (15 mL)” can be replaced on the label with an equivalent unit of measure.

Duration(s) of use

No statement required.

Risk information

Caution(s) and warning(s)

Ask a healthcare practitioner/health care provider/health care professional/doctor/physician if symptoms persist or worsen.

Contraindication(s)

No statement required.

Known adverse reaction(s)

Stop use if hypersensitivity/allergy occurs (ESCOP 2003; Bradley 1992).

Non-medicinal ingredients

Must be chosen from the current Natural Health Products Ingredients Database (NHPID) and must meet the limitations outlined in the database.

Storage conditions

Must be established in accordance with the requirements described in the Natural Health Products Regulations.

Specifications

  • The finished product specifications must be established in accordance with the requirements described in the Natural and Non-Prescription Health Products Directorate (NNHPD) Quality of Natural Health Products Guide.
  • The medicinal ingredient must comply with the requirements outlined in the NHPID.
